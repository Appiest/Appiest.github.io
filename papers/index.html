AI research, one paper a morning

All 21 foundations are out, plus 15 frontier papers. A new paper lands every morning.

2026 1950 2010 2026
Read day 36Yucong Zhang, Cancan Li, Bo Xiao, Ke Zeng, Ming Li (CUHK-Shenzhen, Meituan, Wuhan University)

From Voiceprints to User Entities: Online Speaker-Memory Construction for Multi-User Spoken Agents

A voice assistant shared by several people has to decide whose memory each sentence belongs to before it can personalize anything, and this paper shows that treating a voiceprint as evidence to be carefully updated (rather than a fixed ID card) keeps profiles from splitting apart or bleeding into each other, raising answer accuracy by up to about 12 points with zero cases of handing one user's memories to another.

Origins

  1. Day 1, 1950

    The imitation game

    Instead of arguing about whether machines can think, Turing proposed a test you could actually run: can a machine hold a conversation well enough to pass as a person?

  2. Day 2, 1986

    Backpropagation

    By sending the output error backward through a network with the chain rule, you can train the hidden layers too, and they learn useful features on their own.

Deep learning takes off

  1. Day 3, 2012

    AlexNet

    A big convolutional network trained on two gaming GPUs cut the ImageNet error rate from 26% to 15%, and computer vision switched to deep learning almost overnight.

  2. Day 4, 2013

    word2vec

    Two very simple models learn a vector for every word from raw text in under a day, and the vectors encode meaning well enough that king minus man plus woman lands near queen.

  3. Day 5, 2014

    Seq2seq

    One network reads a sentence into a single vector and a second network writes the translation out of it, and this simple setup matched phrase-based translation systems.

  4. Day 6, 2015

    DQN

    One network, seeing only the screen pixels and the score, learned to play 49 different Atari games, many of them at or above the level of a professional human tester.

  5. Day 7, 2014

    GANs

    Train a forger and a detective against each other, and the forger learns to produce samples that look like the real data.

  6. Day 8, 2015

    ResNet

    Let each block of layers learn only a correction added to its own input, and networks can grow to 152 layers and keep getting better.

The Transformer era

  1. Day 9, 2017

    Transformer

    Drop recurrence entirely and let every word look at every other word at once, and translation gets better and much faster to train.

  2. Day 10, 2018

    BERT

    Teach one Transformer to understand language by filling in hidden words using context from both sides, then lightly retrain that same model for almost any language task and beat specialized systems.

  3. Day 11, 2020

    GPT-3

    Make a next-word predictor big enough (175 billion parameters) and it can pick up new tasks from a few examples written into its prompt, with no retraining at all.

Generative and multimodal

  1. Day 12, 2020

    Diffusion (DDPM)

    Teach a network to remove a little noise from an image, then start from pure static and let it remove noise 1,000 times in a row: what comes out is a brand new, realistic image.

  2. Day 13, 2020

    NeRF

    Store a whole 3D scene inside a small neural network that answers "what color and how solid is the space at this point, seen from this angle?", then render brand new camera views by asking it that question along every ray of light.

  3. Day 14, 2020

    RAG

    Instead of forcing a language model to memorize every fact in its weights, give it a search engine over a library (Wikipedia) and train it to look things up first, then write its answer from what it found.

  4. Day 15, 2021

    CLIP

    Train an image model and a text model together on 400 million captioned pictures from the internet so that matching pictures and words land in the same place, and you get a vision system that can recognize almost anything you can describe in a sentence, with no labeled training data for the task.

Making models useful

  1. Day 16, 2022

    InstructGPT (RLHF)

    A giant language model trained only to predict internet text is not the same as a helpful assistant; show it some human-written examples, have people rank its answers, train a scorer on those rankings, and use reinforcement learning to chase that score, and a model 100 times smaller ends up preferred over the giant.

  2. Day 17, 2022

    Chain-of-Thought

    If the examples in your prompt show a model working through a problem step by step before giving the answer, a big enough language model will write out its own steps too, and that alone roughly triples its accuracy on grade-school math without changing the model at all.

  3. Day 18, 2022

    ReAct

    Let a language model alternate between writing a thought, taking an action like a search, and reading what comes back, and it stops making things up as often and can finish real multi-step tasks; this think, act, observe loop is the skeleton of nearly every AI agent today.

  4. Day 19, 2022

    Whisper

    Train one plain Transformer on 680,000 hours of messy, internet-sourced audio with its existing transcripts, and you get a speech recognizer that works out of the box on almost any recording, in about 100 languages, without being tuned for any of them.

Into the physical world

  1. Day 20, 2023

    3D Gaussian Splatting

    Instead of hiding a 3D scene inside a neural network the way NeRF did, describe it as millions of tiny, soft, colored, see-through blobs that a graphics card can draw directly, and you get NeRF-quality views of real places at over 100 frames per second after under an hour of training.

  2. Day 21, 2023

    RT-2

    Take a big vision-language model that already learned about the world from the internet, teach it to write robot movements as if they were words, and the robot can suddenly handle objects, places and instructions it never practiced on.

Frontier

Day 36

  1. Day 36, 2026

    EvoSpeakerMem (a voice assistant that keeps track of who is who, and whose memories are whose)

    A voice assistant shared by several people has to decide whose memory each sentence belongs to before it can personalize anything, and this paper shows that treating a voiceprint as evidence to be carefully updated (rather than a fixed ID card) keeps profiles from splitting apart or bleeding into each other, raising answer accuracy by up to about 12 points with zero cases of handing one user's memories to another.

Days 29 to 35

  1. Day 35, 2026

    The Trinity (agents that build their own practice worlds and upgrade their own toolkit)

    The Trinity has an AI agent sort dozens of very different tasks by the skills they secretly share, build simple practice worlds for each shared skill, and keep only the upgrades to its own scaffolding that provably help, which lifted one frozen model by about 34 points on its training tasks and about 22 to 26 points on tasks it had never seen.

  2. Day 34, 2026

    DynaFlow (an AI manager that writes its own team plan, or skips the team)

    DynaFlow trains a single "manager" AI to write a small program, fresh for each problem, that assigns work to a team of helper AIs and passes their results between them, and, just as important, to recognize when a problem is easy enough to answer alone.

  3. Day 33, 2026

    StreamVibe (an AI that watches and knows when to speak up)

    StreamVibe teaches a video AI to watch a live stream (your kitchen, your screen) and decide on its own when to help, even when you only said "keep an eye on this" or changed your mind halfway through, by training it on when to stay silent and giving it a notebook that tracks what you currently want.

  4. Day 32, 2026

    FilmFlow (an AI camera crew that plans, shoots, reviews and reshoots)

    FilmFlow turns a one-line shot description like "orbit the dancer while rising, then push in" (or a clip from a real film) into an exact camera move inside a 3D scene, using four AI agents that plan the move from 22 named camera operations, render it, critique the footage, and revise, and viewers ranked its shots best about 6 times out of 10.

  5. Day 31, 2026

    AW-0 (a robot that writes a plan as code, imagines it, then acts)

    AW-0 is one robot model that, in a single quarter-second pass, writes a short program for what it is about to do, imagines a video of doing it, and outputs the motor commands, and making the code come first raised its real-world score from 46 to 71 out of 100 versus the best rival.

  6. Day 30, 2026

    VISAGENT (a phone line run by a marketplace of voice agents)

    China Unicom replaced the press-1-for-this phone menu on a national hotline with one natural voice that listens while it talks, picks the right specialist AI agent out of hundreds in under a second, and hands that agent what it already knows about you.

  7. Day 29, 2026

    Herding in AI agent crowds

    When AI agents can see what earlier agents chose, they pile onto early favorites far more than people do, so a crowd of agents can crown an arbitrary winner and, unlike human crowds, rarely corrects itself.

Days 22 to 28

  1. Day 28, 2026

    MemoryTax (is agent memory worth it?)

    A small, careful study finds that once an AI model is strong enough, specially designed memory systems like Mem0 and Zep stop helping and cost more, while simply keeping the raw conversation and letting the model search it with basic tools scores higher for less money.

  2. Day 27, 2026

    GPT-6-Astra as a robot brain

    A large SenseNova-led team plugged OpenAI's general-purpose GPT-6-Astra, never trained for robots, into 12 robot benchmarks and 8 real robots, and found it beats specialized robot models in simulation on 9 of 12 benchmarks (especially when it supervises a small fast robot model), yet it is still too slow, too expensive and too clumsy with physical contact to be practical in the real world.

  3. Day 26, 2026

    3DCodeVerse (3D worlds built as code)

    Instead of having AI paint 3D objects as frozen meshes or point clouds, have coding agents write short programs that build them, then check those programs by running them, measuring the parts and looking at renders: a USC-led team releases a 2-million-example dataset and a self-checking agent harness that beat Claude Design's 3D objects in 90% of blind human comparisons.

  4. Day 25, 2026

    OpenGCUA (agents that write code to use computers)

    Instead of making an AI agent click through a computer one step at a time, let it write a small program that mixes clicks, typing, terminal commands and checks, and let the model itself decide when it needs to look at the screen: across eight frontier models this raised scores on a hard desktop benchmark by about 20 points and cut the number of steps roughly in half.

  5. Day 24, 2026

    Jeva (fast typed decisions)

    When an AI only needs to pick from a list, say yes or no, or give a score, Jeva treats that as a decision instead of a writing task: it reads each option once, never lets their order matter, can say "none of these," and reuses its work on options it has seen before, which makes it accurate, stable and up to 28 times faster.

  6. Day 23, 2026

    Cortex (context into weights)

    Instead of re-pasting the same notes into a model's prompt forever, Cortex has the model quiz itself on those notes and then bakes the answers into a small add-on to its own weights, so it keeps the new knowledge with the notes gone and barely forgets anything else.

  7. Day 22, 2026

    ATAS (video world memory)

    Give an AI film director a memory of the set's 3D geometry and a time-stamped log of what happened in each scene, only let verified shots into that memory, and its long videos stay consistent when it returns to a place or replays a moment, on top of any video generator.
